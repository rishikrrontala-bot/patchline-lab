# Patchline Lab — customer validation kit

**Blank research kit, 10 October 2026. No interviews, task results, quotes or commitments are populated.** Built by Rishik Rontala, solo. Preparing this kit does not authorize or send outreach.

Discover whether sensor researchers have a repeated quality/evidence task, whether Patchline completes it correctly, and whether someone can buy a narrow pilot. Research examples are not customers. A positive reaction is not a commitment.

## Roles and permission

Initial targets: two daily users (sensor/research engineers or postdoctoral researchers) and one budget owner (PI, lab manager or project lead). Ask about recent work before demonstrating a solution. Keep negative answers. A conversation need not include a file or quote.

Keep permission separate from feedback. Do not accept patient or confidential data for this pilot. Ask the participant to remove identifiers and verify they can share a file. Record retention/deletion preferences and contact. Do not upload their data to a model or another service.

## Interview: 15–20 minutes

1. “Walk me through the last recording you checked and handed to someone else. What instrument/export and software did you use?” Record the actual task, frequency, recipient and outcome.
2. “Where, if anywhere, did that task get difficult? What did you do?” Ask for the last example; include answers that nothing was difficult.
3. “How did you handle missing samples, spikes, clock gaps or an unexplained trend? What evidence did a reviewer need?” Separate known faults from uncertain measurement changes.
4. “Which parts are already solved by your vendor software, spreadsheet or notebook? Why would you keep those?” Record reasons to reject Patchline.
5. “What are the export settings, units, calibration status and maximum recording size?” Request a shareable example only with permission; compatibility is unverified.
6. “How often does this recur, who does the work, and what happened if it was delayed or wrong?” Estimated time/cost is self-report, not observed savings.

Then show a short working flow. Ask which evidence is useful, confusing or unnecessary. Observe an unaided task before pitching price where practical.

## Compare a task fairly

Use permissioned non-patient data with independently reviewed expected readings/findings. Generated fixtures produce **synthetic usability evidence**, not adoption or measured laboratory savings. Injection establishes a known software test; a natural trend does not establish drift. Reference comparisons need documented source, alignment, units and uncertainty.

Prepare two comparable trace sets of similar size, channels and predefined issues. Give equal short training; alternate method order across participants to reduce learning effects. Record order and any limitation. The comparator is the participant's actual current workflow, not an artificially weak baseline.

Task: open/import a recording, identify agreed issues, locate source samples, preserve the original and produce a report a colleague can review. Start timing when opening the first input; stop when saving the complete report. Include mapping, setup and corrections. Record any excluded training separately. A ready chart alone does not finish the task.

Compare elapsed times only when both tasks finish correctly under comparable conditions. Preserve incomplete results. Observed difference = `baseline seconds − Patchline seconds`; divide by positive baseline seconds for percentage difference. State sample size, trace origin, task order and scope. Do not extrapolate one result to every lab or revenue. Monetary value uses a participant-stated labor-cost assumption and remains an estimate. The calculator defaults of eight runs/month, ten minutes saved/run and $30/hour imply only $40 monthly labor value against a $99 price; test frequency and actual time rather than treating this as positive ROI. Labor-only coverage requires twenty such runs, or 24.75 minutes saved on each of eight runs.

## Budget owner and price

Ask who approves a $300 pilot, which budget pays, purchasing/security/licensing constraints, whether contracting with a student is permitted, approval time, the required evidence and who owns the workflow afterward. Ask what would rule it out.

Present the [exact offer](PILOT-OFFER.md), six-hour cap and acceptance criteria. Record the response accurately. Ask what recurring updates/support could justify $99/month, what would make a one-time handoff preferable, and who would approve recurring spending. A grant is not automatically available to us.

Classify evidence precisely: feedback; price reaction; request for a scoped proposal; written commitment with conditions; procurement approval; paid invoice; active use; renewal. Stronger categories need supporting records. Never translate enthusiasm into a sale.

Track acquisition work for unsuccessful prospects too. Observed CAC = attributable sales labor plus cash divided by new paying labs, with period/definition stated; zero acquired labs means CAC is not estimable. Track onboarding separately from acquisition: the calculator adds onboarding hours × labor rate to the acquisition budget/CAC component. Do not include the same work in both inputs. Record support hours, paid starts, losses and renewals. Monthly churn = losses divided by labs paying at period start; zero starting labs makes it undefined. Small samples do not establish stable retention or LTV.

## Decision

Record whether the export works, the task is correct, the pain repeats, a purchasing route exists and work fits the solo time budget. List the strongest rejection and a concrete scope change. Continue to a pilot only if these fit; otherwise narrow or stop the offer. Update [business-validation.md](business-validation.md) with dated evidence and permissioned citations. An unfinished interview plan is still a plan.
